Four places where v2 contradicts the case study

A careful reader will spot these. Each needs a sentence of reasoning, or a change to the app.

Auto-generate on load

Study
Testing killed it in favour of an explicit Generate action.
v2
Every pitch drafts automatically as soon as advisors arrive.

Suggested line: “Drafting by default came back once every statement carried a confidence score. PMs no longer had to trust the draft blind.”

Send gated on approval

Study
The composer loads only after every pitch is approved.
v2
Next is always available, and unsaved pitch edits are lost.

Suggested fix: bring the gate back in the app, or block Next while a pitch is mid-edit and explain the change.

Translation

Study
Translation was surfaced instead of buried.
v2
Translate lives in the editor toolbar, not the header.

Suggested line: say it moved into the editor, beside the text it acts on.

Bulk action toolbar

Study
Generate, regenerate and approve across the shortlist.
v2
Selection happens in the Express List; there's no bulk approve in the builder.

Suggested line: “The whole-shortlist pass now starts in the CRM: pick advisors, send, and every pitch is drafted.”

New chapter · draft copy

Chapter 2 · From fast drafts to trusted drafts

The beta made pitches fast. The workshop showed why PMs still reread them: the model could be confident and wrong. Version two shows the model's confidence and its sources on every claim, so a PM can see where to look instead of checking everything.

2.1

Start where the shortlist starts

Replaces /ai-toolbar.mp4
Bulk-generating inside the builder
→
Pick the shortlist in the CRM and send it to the builder
Section copy

The whole shortlist in one pass, starting in the CRM.

PMs already pick advisors in the Express List. Now that is where the pass begins: select, send, and every pitch arrives drafted. A row opens the full profile without leaving the list.

2.2

Score every statement

Replaces /ai-layout.png
Rereading every line of every pitch
→
Weak claims are underlined and the pitch has a score
Section copy

Show the PM where to look.

Each pitch gets a quality score out of 100, and each statement is underlined by how well the advisor's data supports it. The score updates four seconds after the PM stops typing, so it keeps up without getting in the way.

2.3

Fix it in place, with the receipts

Replaces /ai-rewrite.mp4
Regenerating a pitch and hoping it improves
→
A rewrite for the one weak line, with its sources
Section copy

Every suggestion shows where it came from.

Clicking a flagged line opens a small companion card: what is wrong, a rewrite with the change highlighted, and the screener answers behind it. Apply swaps in the line. Undo is one click, and the card steps aside so the text stays readable.

2.4

Argue from the evidence

New image
Copying profile details into the pitch by hand
→
Select the evidence and generate from it
Section copy

The PM chooses the evidence. The model writes it up.

For unvetted advisors, past screeners and job history are all the pitch can argue from. PMs select the lines that matter, add an optional instruction, and generate bullets into the pitch, or insert them as they are.

2.5

One house style, edited by the PMs

New image
Seven ways of writing the same thing
→
One prompt template and key details per angle
Section copy

The house style is a prompt the PMs can edit.

Discovery found seven ways of writing the same thing. Each angle now has one prompt template and a short list of key details. Editing it regenerates the drafts, and the builder asks before overwriting a pitch someone already edited.

2.6

Review, then send

Replaces /ai-email.png
Composing the client email field by field
→
Land on the finished, branded email: Edit or Send
Section copy

The email arrives finished. The PM decides.

The email step opens on the branded email exactly as the client will get it, with two choices: Edit or Send. Editing adds content blocks the way a notes app does, and a copy button lifts the whole email into Outlook when a PM needs to.

2.7

Say clearly what happened

New image
Section copy

One screen for the outcome, including failure.

After Send, a single screen confirms who received what. If delivery fails, it says nothing reached the client, keeps the email, and offers Try Again.

New section

How autonomous should this be?

Section copy

Level 5 today. A route to 7. A reason to stop there.

On a scale where 1 is fully manual and 10 has no person involved, the builder sits at 5: the AI drafts everything and a person reviews all of it. The next step is approving a finished email once. After that, routine follow-ups could send on their own when every compliance check passes. It stops at 7 because no machine can judge whether an email is right for a particular client. A named Project Manager stays accountable for every first email.

Visual: export the scale and the step cards from the autonomy story. Note the distinction you raised: AI capability is close to 8 because of the companion, but autonomy is 5. Saying both makes the point that trust, not capability, is what's missing.
