How agentic is AI Pitch Builder?

5/ 10 Draft by default

The AI produces the first version of everything: each pitch, the fixes for weak statements and the client email. A person reviews and edits all of it, then presses Send. Nothing reaches a client unless a person has acted on it.

This page explains why it sits at 5, why sending stays with a person for now, and a route to 7 that keeps a named person accountable for every new client conversation.

The scale

1 means a person does everything. 10 means no person is involved.

The level describes how the core work gets done: writing the pitches and the client email. Each step of the workflow also gets its own score, which shows where things are uneven. Switch scenarios to see what changes as the agent takes on more of the work.

Step by step

Where the AI works today, and where it doesn't

Drafting pitches, fixing them and writing the email all sit at 5: the first version is always there before anyone asks. Sending sits at 2, because nothing checks the email before it goes. That gap is where the next level comes from.

Why it stops at Send

A client email has to pass checks that a machine can't fully verify yet

The send step is the one that carries business risk. Every email to a client must meet these rules, which come from the review-agent spike we built and then hid. The machine can already check some of them. The rest are why a person still has to press Send.

The "Yes" rows can be automated now. The "Partly" rows set how far autonomy can go. The "No" row explains why the target stops at 7 and not 10.

The route

From reviewing everything to approving once, then handling only exceptions

At level 5 a person reads and edits every draft. Each move takes some of that review away, but only after the previous move has shown it can be trusted. Pitch rewrites go first, because a change log and an undo button make them low risk.

5 → 6 · Next

Agent prepares the whole email, you approve once

  • High-confidence pitch rewrites are applied automatically, with a change log and undo.
  • Agentic PM turns the request into a proposed shortlist.
  • The compliance checklist runs before Send.
  • One review screen and one approval, then the email sends.

Needs: a visible change log, one-click undo, the "Yes" checks automated, and pitch quality measured against the edits Project Managers actually make.

6 → 7 · Target

Agent handles routine work and escalates the rest

  • Routine follow-ups on existing threads send on their own when every check passes.
  • Anything new, unusual or flagged still waits for a person.
  • Every send has an undo window and an audit log.

Needs: Compliance sign-off, reliable "Partly" checks, an autonomy setting per client or thread type, and agreed rules for escalation.

Not planned · 8+

Why we stop at 7

  • At level 8 the agent would act first and report afterwards, including on new client conversations.
  • Whether an email is right for this client can't be checked by a machine. That judgment is the Project Manager's.
  • A named person stays accountable for every first email to a client.

Revisit when: the "Partly" checks are reliable and clients have agreed to agent-sent email.
